# Presentation prep: AI trade-offs

Solo preparation session for a 20-minute presentation tomorrow (October 16). Four sections, five slides each. The talk covers AI trade-offs for a business audience, drawing on the proximity vs concentration framework and the assistance vs automation distinction.

## Opening hook

Ice shipping from New England to Mumbai: three-quarters of the ice melts en route, but the shipment is still viable through smart packing and shape optimization. One slide on this.

## Core ideas

The proximity vs concentration tradeoff as a framework for thinking about business software. Business software is highly concentrated today -- everyone uses the same tools, and big tech firms hold market power. The term "hyperscaler" needs verification before the talk.

The assistance vs automation tradeoff (credit: Diogo Almeida, CEO, TypeSafe). Automation matters more than assistance in business contexts. This explains adoption patterns: coding works because code was already automation-native. As said in the session: "in coding we have, you know, the assistant can write the code, and the code was already basically a way of automation."

AI features beyond raw intelligence: speed, model size, cost, and control / sovereignty. Pareto frontiers (cost vs intelligence) are already plotted in the AI community thanks to some economist influence. For a business audience, control and automation are the most important dimensions. As said in the session: "if your goal is to build a warehouse, you are not gonna hire Michelangelo to build beautiful marble statues."

## Open models and market dynamics

Open models are catching up fast to closed ones. Source: Epoch AI for benchmarks; Ramp AI for usage data. Price competition is intensifying: newer Gemini and Meta models are very cheap and very capable. These are not only Chinese models -- major Western labs are also pushing open models.

Every firm can now run its own stack: proprietary business logic plus local data plus generic AI algorithms drive business processes. Data sovereignty is a driver, not just cost. Possible angle: European competitiveness in the AI landscape.
