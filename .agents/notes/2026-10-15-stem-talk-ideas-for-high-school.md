# STEM talk ideas for high school

Solo brainstorming session for an invited talk at a STEM high school science festival. The audience is strong in math, programming, and chemistry, but has little economics background. The talk will be in Hungarian.

## Central argument

The core thesis: economic growth is about rearranging atoms, not consuming more of them. Growth comes from combining existing resources in new ways, not from using more resources.

The opening metaphor is a prehistoric human collecting 10 sticks: they can burn them for fire, or combine them into tools and structures. Same number of sticks, exponentially more possibilities. The number of ways to combine $N$ elements grows combinatorially fast, and the magnitude needs to be shown on a log scale to be legible.

As said in the session: "if I consume more atoms I will get fat."

## Examples and illustrations

Sticks lead to a steel truck bridge: the same idea, vastly more sophisticated structure, built from the same elements.

A chemistry slide shows carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen combined in different ways, with wildly different outcomes, for example a toxic compound versus caffeine. This should hook the chemistry-oriented students. The job is to go from toxic to pleasant without taking more atoms, just combining them differently.

The COVID reference works well for this cohort: they lived through school closures and can relate intuitively to exponential growth, for example the SIR model as a real-world nonlinear process.

A mini-rant fits here: mathematical concepts are used sloppily in Hungarian public discourse, like "mathematical chance" (which is just probability between zero and one) or "the growth has become exponential." As noted in the session: "yes or no is the most nonlinear thing" -- said about a linguist colleague who argued for moving away from linear yes-or-no thinking.

## Degrowth rebuttal

The degrowth rebuttal is the planned punchline near the end, after earning it: invoke the law of conservation of matter. Nobody actually consumes atoms; we just rearrange them. The talk should close with students thinking the degrowth economist is the one who has not heard of conservation of matter.

Climate change is not denied: material usage does grow with GDP, but the elasticity of material usage to growth is less than one to one. It would be great if it were zero, and there are leaps of growth that use very little new material, but it is not one to one.

Inequality should be acknowledged but framed as an engine of development, not just a problem.

## Data

Depending on talk length, include charts of World Bank or UN Millennium Development Goals metrics (health, life expectancy) plotted against GDP per capita across countries and over time. Growth consistently drove these gains.

## Talk structure

Possible title: "How can an economy grow forever?" or similar provocative framing. Open with a quote from a famous degrowth economist, deliberately controversial to engage the audience, then build the rebuttal. Keep the tone provocative on purpose.

## Next steps

- Pick specific molecules for the chemistry slide: identify a toxic compound and caffeine (or similar) built from C, H, O, N.
- Work out the combinatorial math: calculate and visualize how many ways 10 sticks or elements can combine, shown on a log scale.
- Select World Bank or development charts for the data section, keeping it tight depending on talk length.
